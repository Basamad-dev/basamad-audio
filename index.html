What we build

Beamforming for speech

Fixed and adaptive beamformers, including the generalized sidelobe canceller, designed for short frame shifts and robust to steering and calibration error.

Source localization

Direction-of-arrival estimation and tracking with SRP-PHAT, GCC-PHAT and subspace methods, tuned for reverberant rooms.

Echo cancellation

Multi-channel acoustic echo cancellation with partitioned-block frequency-domain filters, double-talk control and residual echo suppression.

Multi-talker separation

Spatial separation of simultaneous talkers, from two people at a table to a crowded room.

Real-time implementation

Floating-point and fixed-point C/C++ with explicit latency, memory and CPU budgets from the first design review.

Evaluation

Simulated rooms with known sources, scored with SIR, SI-SDR, ERLE, PESQ and STOI, so every claim has a measurement behind it.

How a project runs

  1. Define the acoustic problem: array or loudspeaker layout, room, latency limit and target hardware.
  2. Prototype the algorithm in Python against simulated and recorded scenes.
  3. Validate with objective metrics and listening tests, and agree the pass criteria with you.
  4. Deliver an implementation-ready design or C/C++ code, plus the test setup that reproduces the results.
